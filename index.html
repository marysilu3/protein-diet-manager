7-day protein-rich soup meal plan

Have one soup as part of the evening meal each day. These are meal ideas, not a soup-only diet: continue suitable breakfast and lunch foods from the Kerala meal plan and food list. Keep the texture soft and remove bones. Portions and protein totals depend on the recipe.

Day 1 — Chicken

Evening soup: Thick chicken soup with finely shredded, fully cooked chicken.

Serve with: Soft rice kanji or idiyappam, as tolerated.

Tip: Include the chicken pieces; clear strained broth alone has little protein.

Day 2 — Egg

Evening soup: Egg drop soup with a fully cooked egg stirred into warm broth.

Serve with: Soft dosa or a small portion of rice.

Tip: Ensure the egg is thoroughly cooked.

Day 3 — Green gram

Evening soup: Green gram soup, cooked until very soft and lightly mashed or blended.

Serve with: Soft rice or kanji.

Tip: Keep the lentils in the soup rather than straining them out.

Day 4 — Fish

Evening soup: Fish soup with boneless, well-cooked fish flakes.

Serve with: Soft rice kanji.

Tip: Check carefully for every bone before serving.

Day 5 — Dal and pumpkin

Evening soup: Lentil-pumpkin soup, with dal cooked until soft and blended if preferred.

Serve with: Idli or soft rice.

Tip: Dal provides more protein than pumpkin or vegetable broth alone.

Day 6 — Soy and vegetables

Evening soup: Soy-vegetable soup with soft, fully cooked soy pieces.

Serve with: A small portion of rice or chapati if suitable.

Tip: Use a familiar soy product and a texture that is easy to chew.

Day 7 — Chickpea

Evening soup: Black chickpea soup, pressure-cooked until very soft and blended or mashed.

Serve with: Soft kanji or puttu softened with curry.

Tip: Start with a comfortable portion if legumes cause gas or bloating.

Lower-protein soup choices

Tomato soup, plain mixed-vegetable soup, mushroom soup made without milk, and strained bone broth are generally lower in protein.

Use these for variety, but pair them with egg, chicken, fish, dal, green gram, chickpeas or milk if these foods are allowed.

Important: Do not use soup as the only food for the day. After surgery, confirm the protein target, fluid advice and allowed foods with the treating doctor or dietitian. If kidney function is reduced, or there are swallowing restrictions, follow the clinician's specific advice.

Low-fibre food guide

Use this guide only if the surgeon or dietitian has advised a temporary low-fibre / low-residue diet. It is not automatically required after every operation, and it should not be continued longer than advised. Introduce foods and portions according to the care team's instructions.

Rice, grains and starch

  • White rice and soft rice kanji (വെളുത്ത അരി, കഞ്ഞി)
  • Idli, plain dosa, idiyappam
  • Rava / semolina (റവ), refined white bread
  • Peeled boiled potato (തൊലി കളഞ്ഞ ഉരുളക്കിഴങ്ങ്)

Avoid brown rice, bran, wholegrain breads, seeds and coarse cereals during a strict low-fibre phase.

Protein foods

  • Well-cooked egg (മുട്ട)
  • Boneless, soft fish (മീൻ)
  • Soft, finely shredded chicken (കോഴിയിറച്ചി)
  • Milk, smooth curd or yoghurt if tolerated
  • Smooth paneer if tolerated

Keep protein in the diet if allowed. Avoid dal, green gram, chickpeas and other pulses during a strict low-fibre phase unless the clinician permits them.

Vegetables — peel, deseed and cook very soft

  • Carrot (കാരറ്റ്)
  • Pumpkin (മത്തങ്ങ)
  • Potato without skin (തൊലി കളഞ്ഞ ഉരുളക്കിഴങ്ങ്)
  • Beetroot, well cooked and peeled (ബീറ്റ്റൂട്ട്)
  • Bottle gourd (ചുരയ്ക്ക)
  • Ash gourd (കുമ്പളങ്ങ)

Serve in small portions. Mash or blend if the prescribed texture requires it. Tolerance varies.

Fruits — ripe, peeled and without seeds

  • Ripe banana (പഴുത്ത പഴം)
  • Ripe papaya, seeds removed (പപ്പായ)
  • Peeled, stewed apple (തൊലി കളഞ്ഞ് വേവിച്ച ആപ്പിൾ)
  • Peeled, stewed pear (പിയർ)
  • Soft canned peach or pear without skin or seeds

Start with a small portion. Avoid fruit skins, seeds, pips and dried fruit.

Foods to avoid initially on a strict low-fibre diet

  • Raw salads, vegetable peels, stalks and stringy vegetables
  • Leafy greens, cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli and sweetcorn
  • Beans, peas, lentils, green gram and chickpeas
  • Guava, pomegranate, pineapple, berries and fruits with seeds
  • Coconut, nuts, seeds, dates, raisins and other dried fruits

This is a temporary guide, not a permanent forbidden-food list. Reintroduce fibre gradually only when the surgeon or dietitian says it is appropriate.

Soup choices

During a strict low-fibre phase, consider smooth chicken, egg or boneless-fish soup if permitted. Strain out vegetable skins, seeds and coarse pieces only if the prescribed diet requires a smooth texture.

Dal, green gram, chickpea and soy soups contain fibre and may not suit a strict low-fibre phase. Plain vegetable or strained broth may also provide little protein.

Safety reminder: Low fibre does not mean low protein. However, the right protein amount and food texture depend on surgery, bowel function, kidney function and other medical conditions. Follow the treating team's advice, especially for bowel narrowing, ongoing vomiting, abdominal swelling, pain, constipation or diarrhoea.

Health note

This app is a food-recording aid, not a medical prescription. For surgery recovery, kidney disease, abnormal creatinine, swallowing difficulty or a prescribed special diet, confirm the protein target and food choices with the treating doctor or dietitian. Food values are approximate.
